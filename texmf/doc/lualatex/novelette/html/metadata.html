Overview

Important: Novelette does not use the modern \DocumentMetadata setting. If you use it, an error results. This is not a bug. It is due to the difference between the needs for print-to-paper books, and assistive Ebooks.

You must provide the book title and author. You may also provide a subtitle. If your book has more than one edition, or if you are concerned about version control, then you may set version and docdate.

In draft mode, you may omit some or all metadata. Athough this is bad practice, it may be useful if you have not yet decided on the book's title, or other information. Novelette will provide generic defaults. But you cannot proceed to preview or final mode until you have set the title and author.

In draft and preview modes, Novelette will automatically edit metadata, so that the PDF is not confused with the final result. This will also happen if you request final mode, but it is prevented by error or warning.

In final mode, when a PDF compiles without error or warning, it can be PDF/X-1a:2001 format. Even though this format is old, it is preferred by many print-on-demand services, because it is the closest to "digital paper" that PDF can provide.

The PDF will always have an old-fashioned Info dictionary. You may choose whether or not it also has XMP metadata. If your book has a subtitle, you may choose whether the Info and XMP have a subtitle entry, or not. These choices may depend on the requirements of your print service.

 

Allowed Characters

TOP

All characters in metadata must be in the Latin-1 character set. No exceptions. Although some specialty distributors allow more, Novelette does not.

Not all Latin-1 characters are allowed. This table shows the subset that Novelette will accept. Particularly note that curly quotes, and dashes (other than ordinary hyphen) are not in Latin-1.

 

Title and Subtitle

TOP

\title{Title of Your Book}
\subtitle{Optional Subtitle}

  • Title is required. Subtitle is optional.

  • Novelette restricts the total number of characters in title plus subtitle to about 200. That's a lot more than you need for popular fiction. Some distributors prefer that the total not exceed 75 characters.

  • In draft mode, you may omit the title. Novelette provides a default. But in preview and final modes, you must provide the title.

 

Author (Real or Pseudonym)

TOP

\author{Author Name}

  • The author name is as-spoken: Jane Smith, not Smith, Jane.

  • Novelette has no provision for multiple authors, but you may write Jane Smith and John Jones, if permitted by the distributor.

  • Important: if you use a pseudonym, be sure to use your real name as copyright holder, on the copyright page. If the author is Joe Fakename and someone infringes the copyright, then you will need to provide that you, Jack Realname, have the right to enforce that copyright. Yes, this can actually happen. Although the database can match the two names, that is an extra step, and extra trouble.

  • A pseudonym will not conceal your real name from those who wish to know it.

 

Same Metadata Everywhere

TOP

  • Print-on-demand requires that the title, subtitle, and author that you register with the distribution database, be the same as they appear anywhere in your book.

    If the title is Twenty-Five Thousand Ways to Catch Crocodiles, that is too long to print in page headers (even if it fits, it would be distracting). You cannot abbreviate it as 25000 Ways or Catching Crocs.

    If the author is Aloysius Presumptuous Morgenthanger, the distributor might possibly allow you to use A. P. Morgenthanger in page headers and other places. But do not rely on this!

  • For visual effect, you may change case. If the title is Win Whenever, you may show it as WIN WHENEVER in print. The database is case-insensitive.

    If page headers show the title, they do not need to show the subtitle. But if the title is Magma, subtitle The Garnering, then you might use Magma : The Garnering because it is short enough.

  • If the distributor insists that you change something so that all of the metadata matches, do not complain. Obey.

 

Version and Docdate

TOP

\version{text}
\docdate{yyyy/mm/dd}

These two settings are optional. They are not registered with the distribution database. Their only purpose is for your own version control, if you are trying different plot lines, or your book is being reprinted with changes.

  • Every PDF contains two hex-code strings that serve as identifiers. In principle, the unique identifier remains unchanged, even when there are minor edits; the per-instance identifier changes whenever there are edits.

  • But that is "in principle." In reality, depending on various factors, standard LaTeX may simply create two new hex strings, every time the LaTeX document is produced to PDF, even if nothing has changed. Then, the hex strings are useless for version control.

  • Novelette creates the unique identifier from a hash of the title, subtitle, author, and docdate (but not the version). As long as those remain unchanged, the unique identifier will be the same.

  • The per-instance identifier hashes all of the metadata, including version.

Syntax for \version:

  • \version{text} has no particular format for its text. It could be left empty (default), or be a number such as 1.4, or a more elaborate string such as After surfing on July 14. As long as the characters are allowed, Novelette does not care.

  • If your book has multiple editions, or is printed in several sizes: Best practice is to set the version so that it describes the edition, such as \version{Second Edition, B-Format, rev 6}.

Syntax for \docdate:

  • \docdate{yyyy/mm/dd} should be the date on which you began work on this document, not the current date. A book with docdate 2024-08-23 retains that date, even if it is not finished until months later.

    If you re-write a story that others have already seen, then change the docdate for the revision. That helps to identify which came first.

  • There is no legal significance to the docdate. Novelette does not check its value, only its format as yyyy/mm/dd. A docdate of 2056-14-43 makes no sense, but will be allowed.

 

PDF/X

TOP

\pdfx{setting}

Many print-on-demand services request PDF/X-1a:2001 format. This requires everything that a printed book needs, and disallows anything that a printed book cannot use. Although it is useless for modern Ebooks, it is the closest to "digital paper" that PDF an provide.

For example: If a PDF contains hyperlinks, your home or office printer will print it without complaint. But it will be rejected by your book print service, because hyperlinks serve no purpose in print, and different printers would render them differently.

Novelette has built-in support for PDF/X-1a:2001 format. It is automatically applied in final mode, when the document comples without error or warning. Otherwise, it is not applied. You may adjust its properties with the \pdfx{setting} command in Preamble:

  • \pdfx{fogra} is the default for all languages except American English. This sets the Output Intent to FOGRA39, a generic CMYK print condition.

  • \pdfx{swop} is the default for American English. This sets the Output Intent to CGATS TR 001, better known as U.S. Web Coated (SWOP) v2. This is a similar, generic CMYK print condition.

  • \pdfx{off} disables PDF/X-1a:2001 format. Only use this when requested by the distributor.

  • Although the print condition is CMYK, Novelette only uses black ink, (0C 0M 0Y 100K). If your book has grayscale images, the printer will emulate grays by raster dot patterns, not by gray ink.

  • No color profile (*.icc) is attached or named.

 

XMP Metadata

TOP

\enable{xmp}

Modern Ebooks, and similar digital publications, place metadata in a special XMP block. This is human-readable, UTF8 encoded XML format, and is easily read by database search. It can contain much more useful information than the older PDF Info dictionary, which is not encoded as UTF8.

Readers do not see your PDF file. The distribution database contains whatever is needed for search, using the terms that your provide when you register your book. Thus, XMP is not relevant for print. Also, the PDF/X-1a:2001 format preceded the use of XMP.

If your distributor requests XMP, use \enable{xmp} in Preamble. This retains the Info dictionary (required) and adds the XMP metadata block.

When distributors request "without metadata", they mean that you must not use XMP.
