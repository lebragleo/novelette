Overview

The metadata settings are \title \subtitle \author \version \docdate. The characters allowed in these settings are a subset of Latin‑1.

Special Cases

^TOP

What Novelette allows, what book services allow, and what you should do, may not perfectly agree. Here are some rules and suggestions:

  • If you use any of # & @ characters, they must be escaped by backslash: \# \& \@. If you use these characters, then they must be consistently used wherever the title, subtitle, or author appear. If you register The Three-Dollar Thief as title, then you cannot print The $3 Thief on the cover, title or copyright pages, or page headers.

  • Except as described above, you cannot use the backslash character for any purpose. No macros. No codes such as \'E for Eacute.

  • The middot (periodcentered) character may only be used if necessary in Catalan, and only if your distributor allows it. Avoid this character, if possible.

  • Novelette disallows monetary units and percent, even though some distributors allow them. In general, books with those symbols are nonfiction investment advice, which is not a Novelette genre. You may spell the words: The Three-Dollar Thief or Eighty Percent Good.

  • The semicolon is allowed, but discouraged, as it is likely to cause confusion. The only well-known book with a semicolon in its title is Moby-Dick; or, The Whale.

  • The asterisk is sometimes used as a trick for bad language: Sh*tty Story. The only well-known book with asterisk in its title (long before it became a TV series) is M*A*S*H.

  • The colon is normally used only to separate title from subtitle, when they appear on the same line. If you use This : That as title, it may be interpreted as title This with subtitle That. Certain to cause confusion, if there is also a separate subtitle.

  • The numbersign is normally used only to indicate books in a series, such as The Blonk (Potboiler #5).

  • The forward slash is sometimes used to indicate alternate titles, for example when there are two short books printed as one book. If the metadata needs a fraction, best practice is to spell it out: Three-Fourths, not 3/4.

  • If you use parentheses, they must (properly match).

  • No quotes of any kind. The quotesingle character may be used only as an apostrophe, and that's a fact.

  • No guillemets.

  • You cannot use -- or --- to create dashes.

  • For inverted exclamation and question marks, paste the characters from a Unicode character map. Do not LaTeX codes.

  • The Novelette developer does not know whether a French book titled Zut! should use Zut! or Zut ! in metadata. If you must provide space, use the ordinary space character, not a non‑breaking space.
