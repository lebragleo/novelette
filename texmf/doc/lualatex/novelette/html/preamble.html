Preamble

Overview   All Possible Settings

Overview

A minimal Preamble contains nothing but \title{Book Title} and \author{Author Name}. In draft mode (default), you do not even need those! Novelette will use defaults for a book in English, with trim width 5.5in and trim height 8.5in.

If your language is English, better results will be obtained if you choose either en‑US or en‑UK. If not English, then you must set the language, or hyphenation will be wrong.

 

All Possible Settings

Even a large Preamble will not contain many lines. Click on a command to see its syntax and possible settings:

\documentclass{novelette}
\mode{choice}
\title{Book Title}
\subtitle{Optional Subtitle}
\author{Author Name}
\docdate{yyyy/mm/dd}
\version{Version String}
\lang{language code}
\trimsize{width=W,height=H}
\layout{lines=L,pagestyle=P,glue=G,cpl=C}
\pdfx{choice}
\headstyle{font=F,scale=S,track=T,deco=D,case=C}
\namestyle{font=F,scale=S,track=T,align=A,case=C}
\subnamestyle{font=F,scale=S,track=T,align=A,case=C}
\scenestyle{align=A}
\footnotestyle{indent=I}
\loadfont{comma,separated,choices}
\enable{comma,separated,choices}
\disable{comma,separated,choices}
\examine{0|1}
% No settings for margins and font size!
% No added packages!
\begin{document}

 

\documentclass{novelette}

TOP

There are no class options! All configuration is performed by commands in Preamble.

At some future time, there may be an option that invokes experimental or reversion code.

 

\mode{choice}

TOP

Choices: draft preview final. Default draft.

The purpose of modes is to allow faster compiling while you are writing and editing your book, and avoid confusion between good and bad results.

  • Whichever mode you choose, the typesetting (line breaks, page breaks) will be the same.

  • In draft mode, placeholder rectangles appear instead of images. They will occupy the same space and float position. The graphics page has details..

  • If the compiler issues any warning, error, or bad box, then the mode reverts to draft.

  • Use preview mode for privately sending the PDF to a reviewer or editor. It shows all images, and performs all tests, same as final mode.

  • In draft and preview modes, the metadata is changed, so that the PDF cannot be mistaken for your final product.

  • Use final mode when you are ready to upload the PDF to your print service.

 

Metadata: Title, Subtitle, Author.

TOP

Important: Novelette does not use the \DocumentMetadata command.

  • \title{Book Title} is required in preview and final modes. In draft mode, you may omit it (generic default is provided).

  • \subtitle{Book Subtitle} is optional. Default empty.

    If you use \title{Title : Subtitle}, then do not use the separate \subtitle setting.

  • \author{Author Name} is required in preview and final modes. In draft mode, you may omit it (generic default is provided).

    The author name is as-spoken: John Smith, not Smith, John. You may use a pseudonym, but use your real name as copyright holder, on the copyright page.

  • If title+subtitle+author has a very large number of characters, Novelette will complain. Print services may insist that title+subtitle not exceed a limit, such as 75 characters.

  • The title, subtitle, and author must exactly match the data that you provide when you register your book with the print service. For example, if the registered title is This and That, it cannot appear in metadata as This \& That.

  • If your book is a collection of short stories, create a short title that describes the common theme.

  • All metadata settings must obey these rules:

    • All characters must be in a subset of Latin‑1.

    • If you use any of @ # & characters, they must be escaped with backslash: \@ \# \&.

    • Otherwise, you may not use the backslash for any purpose. In particular, you may not use codes such as \'E for Eacute. Instead, you must paste the actual characters from a Unicode character map.

    • Novelette tests your metadata for compliance. Fatal error if bad characters.

 

Metadata: Version Control.

TOP

Each PDF contains two ID hash strings. In principle, the uniqueID identifies the document, and remains unchanged with minor edits. The instanceID is changed with each significant revision. This is useful for version control.

Standard LaTeX does not obey those rules: The hash strings are changed, every time the document is compiled, whether or not any changes were made. Useless for version control.

Novelette can help. The uniqueID is hashed from title, subtitle, author, and two special settings: \docdate and \version. When all of them are unchanged, the uniqueID remains fixed. The instanceID will still change with each compile, whether or not anything is changed.

  • \docdate and \version are optional. Default empty.

  • \docdate{yyyy/mm/dd} uses only the yyyy/mm/dd format. The format is checked, but its value is not checked: You may use a silly date such as 2056/93/42 without error. Do not do that!

    The docdate should be the day on which you began writing your book. Do not change it unless your revision is so substantial that it is effectively a different story. There is no legal significance to the date, but it will be shown as CreationDate in PDF metadata.

  • \version{text string} has no particular format. Write what you wish. If the book has different editions, or is printed at different sizes, be sure to change the version.

    For your own benefit, best practice is to change the version from time to time, as your writing progresses. Perhaps 1.1 build 2345 or something similar.

  • You must obey the same character rules, as for title/subtitle/author. They are described above.

 

Hyphenation Language: \lang{code}

TOP

Novelette does not load the babel or polyglossia packages.

  • The \lang{code} setting only selects hyphenation patterns. It does not select spelling, date/time format, or anything else.

  • Available languages are Western European. There are no plans to extend Novelette to other world languages.

  • The language codes are listed here. Some languages have special tricks, so be sure to read that page.

  • Novelette is monolingual. Only one hyphenation pattern is used throughout. Your text may contain words and phrases in any language supported by the fonts, but it will be hyphenated (badly) according to the language setting.

  • If you do not set the language, Novelette provides generic American English. But this is not best, even if you are American. Choose the correct en-US or en-UK setting, for better hyphenation.

 

\trimsize{width=W,height=H}

TOP

Trim size is the physical, finished size of book interior pages. Print‑on‑demand softcover uses the "Prefect Print" process, which exactly fits the cover to the interior. Traditional hardcover binding uses a slightly larger cover.

  • The width and height are measured with units of inches or millimeters. The same units must be used for both. Use in for inches, mm for millimeters.

  • Use only standard sizes offered by your print service, with off‐white (cream, creme) paper. Never use white paper for popular fiction.

  • Avoid "extended distribution" (whatever it is named). You do not want booksellers to be ordering copies at your cost if they are unsold.

  • Height must exceed width by a certain amount.

  • Among the American stadard sizes, the smallest allowed by Novelette is width=5in,height=8in and the largest is width=6in,height=9in.

  • Among the British standard sizes, the smallest allowed by Novelette is width=129mm,height=198mm (B-format), and the largest is width=156,height=234 (Royal).

  • American print services often list width before height, but British print services often list height before width. In the \trimsize command, the order does not matter.

  • No cheating! Novelette fails if you attempt to use a trim size outside its limits.

  • Default trim size is width=5.5in,height=8.5in, which is often used for such books in the USA. If you are American, this is probably your best choice. The closest British size is width=138mm,height=216mm (Demy).

  • Web sites may claim that "most books" are 6in x 9in or larger. But that is because "most books" are either nonfiction how‑to manuals, textbooks, or hardcover library books. Softcover popular fiction is rarely that large.

 

\layout{lines=L,pagestyle=P,glue=G,cpl=C}

TOP

This determines the general layout of page headers/footers and main text. Novelette automatically sets the margins and main font size; you cannot set them manually.

  • L is an integer from 28 to 35. Default 35.

    This sets the lines per page, in main textblock (not including header/footer).

  • P is one of plain split center margin marcen. Default marcen.

    This determines whether layout has page header, footers, or both. See the header/footer layouts for illustrations.

  • G is either normal or wide Default normal.

    Normally, Novelette reserves an extra width of 0.125in (3.2mm) at the spine margin, for binding glue. If your book has many pages (such as 300), the wide setting will make the reserved area 50% wider.

  • C is a number from 62 to 72. May be decimal. Default 66.

    In English, and probably most other Western European languages, flowing text is most readable when the lines do not contain too many or too few characters.

    This sets the average number of characters per line. It is an estimate. Only change the value if your writing style must accomodate many long words, or if you need to print more or fewer pages. If in doubt, do not set it.

 

PDF/X-1a:2001 Format: \pdfx{choice}

TOP

Novelette has built-in capability for PDF/X-1a:2001 format. This is preferred by many print-on-demand services. It is the closest to "digital paper" that PDF can provide.

  • The choice is one of fogra swop off, known as Output Intent.

  • If your language is American English (default, or en-US) then the default choice is swop. For all other languages, including British English, the default choice is fogra.

  • swop refers to CGATS TR 001, better known as "U.S. Web Coated (SWOP) v2".

  • fogra refers to FOGRA39.

  • off disables PDF/X, by omitting its required metadata. Otherwise, it prints the same.

  • The PDF/X-1a:2001 specification was created for use in color offset printing. Its Output intent (swop or fogra) is irrelevant for your purposes, and will be ignored by the print service. But since it is part of the spec, it must be provided, unless you do not want PDF/X-1a:2001.

  • Novelette does not name or attach an icc color profile. Ink is 100K (black) only. Grayscale images are rasterized by the printer, using methods that are beyond your control.

 

Page header Style

TOP

The \layout setting determines whether or not there are page headers and/or footers. The \headstyle command determines how they appear. This is a global setting with no local over-ride, although header/footer does not print in some locations.

\headstyle{font=F,scale=S,track=T,deco=D,case=C}

  • F is a font code. Default main.

  • S is a decimal number 0.88 - 1. Default 0.94.

  • T is an integer 0-9. Tracking, from tight to very loose. Default 2.

  • D is one of none bar square bullet lozenge.

    Default varies with font and layout.

    If layout pagestyle is center, then none is actually a slash.

  • C is one of none titl smcp onum smon. Default none.

    Ignored unless header font is main.

    • none No changes. Default.

    • titl Lowercase letters become titling caps.

    • smcp Lowercase letters become small caps.

    • onum Numerals become oldstyle.

    • smon Combines smcp and onum.

 

Default \name Style

TOP

The \name command is one way to style text, in display zones. This sets the default appearance. You may locally over-ride any of these, by per-instance option of the \name command.

\namestyle{font=F,scale=S,track=T,align=A,case=C}

  • F is a font code. Default main.

  • S is a decimal number 1-4. Default 2.

    If followed by a (such as 2.7a) it will auto-adjust slightly wide content.

  • T is an integer 0-9. Tracking, tight-loose. Default 2.

  • A is one of center left right. Default center.

  • L is one of none above below both. Default none.

    Prints decorative line(s) above/below text, at its width.

  • C is one of none titl smcp onum smon. Default none.

    Ignored unless font is main.

    • none No changes. Default.

    • titl Lowercase letters become titling caps.

    • smcp Lowercase letters become small caps.

    • onum Numerals become oldstyle.

    • smon Combines smcp and onum.

 

Default \subname Style

TOP

This has the same purpose, and same syntax, as the \namestyle command, shown above. The only diference is that \subnamestyle{choices} applies to the \subname command, and the default scale is 1.25.

 

Default \scene Style

TOP

This sets the global alignment of the \scene command. There is no local over-ride.

\scenestyle{align=A}.

  • A is one of left right center. Default left.

 

Default \footnote Style

TOP

This sets the appearance at the start of each footnote. If a footnote has more than one paragraph, the additional paragraphs are indented normally.

\footnotestyle{indent=I}

  • I is one of near away both none. Default near.

    • near Text at indent, label and space just before it.

    • away Label at margin, big space, text at indent.

    • both Label at indent, space, text.

    • none Label at margin, space, text.

 

\loadfont{comma,separated,choices}

TOP

Novelette always loads the NoveletteSerif fonts. It only loads its other fonts upon request. This saves time and space.

If you choose fonts in any of the above-listed styling commands, then Novelette will automatically load those fonts.

If your book will use a font that is not NoveletteSerif and is not among the fonts loaded by style settings, then you must tell Novelette to load them now, so that they are available for later use.

\loadfont{comma,separated,codes}. See font codes

 

\enable{comma,separated,choices}

TOP

Each of these choices enables some optional behavior:

  • xmp Adds an XMP metadata packet. The technology used by Novelette stores metadata in a PDF Info dictionary, because it is required by the long-established standard for print-on-demand. If your print service requests XMP metadata, then enable it. If the service asks for PDF "without metadata" they mean that you should not enable this.

  • guide=G G is an integer, from 0 to lines per page. This places a guide marker in the left margin, at the indicated line number. The guide is only placed on the first page of each component. Value 0 means no guide.

    This is useful for vertically aligning the start of main text, beneath new chapter displays. The marker is "g" with an arrow pointing to the correct text baseline. Note that this does not force the text position; you must align it yourself.

  • britq If you type ``` or ''' then Novelette will interpret the triple characters as American-style quotes: double on the outside, single inside, with a narrow nonbreaking space between them.

    When you enable britq, this interpretation is reversed, to single outside, double inside. Note that isolated quotes are not transformed single<‑>double. The interpetation only applies to triple characters.

    Note: Unlike other LaTeX, Novelette does not change " to curly quotes. See the quotes page for details.

 

\disable{comma,separated,choices}

TOP

Each of these choices disables some standard behavior:

  • guide Hides all guides, whether global or local. Note that guides only print in draft mode.

  • renumber Normally, Novelette begins frontmatter with lowercase roman numeral i, then resets to arabic 1 at mainmatter. This is standard book design. The PDF page numbers (seen in the menu bar of a PDF viewer) will not be the same as the printed page numbers. If you disable renumber, then arabic numerals will be used throughout, from 1 at frontmatter, without reset at mainmatter. Then, the PDF viewer shows page numbers the same as they will print.

  • blankend Most print services require PDF with an even number of pages, with the last page entirely blank. Novelette automatically does that. When you disable blankend, no pages are added.

  • pageflip Only obeyed in draft mode. Normally, the side margins shift from left to right, due to the spine glue strip width. That causes the textblock to shift back and forth, which is distracting when you edit. If you disable pageflip, the glue width is split, so the textblock does not shift. Typesetting is unaffected: same line and page breaks.

  • autodeco The transition frontmatter/mainmatter requires that the final page of frontmatter be blank verso. Normally, you do this yourself. But if Novelette detects that your frontmatter ends with non‑blank verso, it will automatically insert a decorative recto page, followed by blank verso. Also applies at mainmatter/backmatter transition. When you disable autodeco, no pages are inserted.

    Best practice is to allow autodeco. When you review your PDF, you will notice if the decorative page was added. You might decide that you like it! But if you do not like it, it is better to create your own page there, instead of disabling autodeco.

  • metasub If your book has a subtitle, Novelette creates a custom PDF Info dictionary entry for that purpose. And if you enable XMP, the subtitle is written using Prism namespace. If your print service does not like that, disable metasub to remove Info and Prism metadata for subtitle.

 

\examine{0 or 1}

TOP

The wonderful lua-typo code, by Daniel Flipo, can inspect each PDF page after it has been typeset, looking for typographic flaws. Those are not errors. They are places where something is not as good-looking is it should be.

Novelette has a modified version of that code. This capability is off, by default. You may turn it on with \examine{1}. Details of what it does, and why you should care, are described here.

 

No Margins?! No Font Size?!

TOP

There are no settings for margins or font size! Novelette automatically calculates them, for best appearance with your chosen (or deful) trim size and page layout.

The smallest available sizes have margins at least 0.375in (10mm) plus 0.125in (3.2mm) extra at the spine, for glue. The medium to large trim sizes increase the margins to at least 0.5in (13mm), plus spine glue. These values are acceptable to nearly all print services.

If your book must have at least 0.5in margins (plus spine glue), then choose a trim size that is at least US 5.5in wide, 8.5in high, or British Demy 138mm wide, 216mm high.

The calculated main font size will vary with trim size, layout, and possibly some other settings.

 

No Packages?!

TOP

Novelette is self-contained. You cannot add any packages.

There is a close interaction between Novelette LaTeX code, its Lua support, and its fonts. Anything else is likely to cause problems.

Novelette does what you need to do, for its intended genres. It is for writers, not programmers.

 

\begin{document}

TOP

Novelette does many things with this command.

When you write various settings in Preamble, most of them have no immediate effect. Your typing is checked for syntax and sanity, then the values are stored.

When the document body begins, Novelette calculates margins and font sizes, based on your settings and defaults. It loads the fonts. It sets a number of internal variables that will be needed later.

This behavior differs from other LaTeX document classes, which read many settings as a class option, and uses packages to load fonts.
