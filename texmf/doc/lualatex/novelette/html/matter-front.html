Novelette Documentation - Front Matter

Overview  

Overview

Front matter precedes your story. It contains information about the book: title, author, copyright, and often other things that are not part of the story itself.

Professional nonfiction often has extended front matter, where the author (or others) explain why you should read this book, and why you should believe what you read. Or, in the case of reprinted classical material, there may be critical or historical essays there.

Popular fiction almost never has extended front matter. Get to the story! A good minimum for front matter is six (6) pages: Half-Title, Frontispiece (often blank), Title Page, Copyright Page, Transitional Page (often a Dedication or Epigraph), blank page. Then the main matter (the story) begins on the next page, which is always recto.

Disabling Page Reset: \disable{pagereset}

In the PDF, all pages are numbered from 1, without reset. In print, it is usually the case that front matter pages are numbered from lowercase roman i, but these numbers do not appear unless the frontmatter is extended. Then, the printed page numbering is reset to arabic 1 at main matter. This means that page numbers visible in print, are not the same as their page numbers in the PDF.

If you wish to have the PDF page numbers be the same as their printed numbers, write \disable{pagereset} in Preamble. Then, page numbers are arabic throughout, without reset. It will still be the case that some pages will not show their page numbers.

Using \frontmatter

Write \frontmatter immediately after \begin{document}. Once used, repeated \frontmatter commands will be ignored.

Half-Title Page

It is customary to use a "half-title" as first page in frontmatter. Historically, this was a dust cover for books that were printed, but not yet bound. It remains useful, as a way to protect the title page, and as a place where readers can get your autograph or write a note.

The half-title page contains nothing but the title. No author. Usually no subtitle. It should be styled the same as on the title page, but with smaller text. You may use an image.

Frontispiece

The second page of front matter has no particular purpose. In the era of great books, it might show artwork related to the book's theme. Nowadays, it is likely to contain a list of books by the same author, or an advertisement for some product. Most often, it is simply blank.

  • Most print-on-demand services discourage a list of books by the same author, unless they are the service for all of them.

  • This is not the place to write about yourself, or your inspiration and experiences for writing the story!

    • If you have high-quality line art related to the story's theme, you may put it here. But avoid poor drawings. Photographs can be used, but they will not print well with the technology used for your book, so avoid them.

  • You may place a map, a diagram of the crime scene, a family history, a drawing of one of the scenes, or something else related to the story. But only place it here, if it is an inessential decorative item, rather than something the reader must view to understand the story.

  • If in doubt, leave this page blank: \blankpage

Title Page

The third page in front matter is the title page. It contains the title, subtitle, and author. Often, it contains the name and location of the publisher at the bottom of the page. Remember that the publisher of a self-published book is you, not the print service.

Best practice is to make a visually interesting title page, possible rendering some of it as a fancy image. Do not make it so fancy that the title is hard to read.

Sample title page:

\begin{component}
\blanline\blankline\blankline
\blankline\blankline
\name{THE}\name{CONSPIRACY}\name{MANIFESTO}
\blankline
\subname{LONDON}
\blankline\blankline\blankline
\name{Jack DeRipper}
\vfill % forces next content to bottom
\begin{block}[align=center]
Indescribably Bad Press • Honking, New Zealand
\end{block}
\end{component}

Copyright Page

The fourth page in front matter is the copyright page. Different jurisdictions and print services have requirements for what must or must not appear on this page. Novelette does not know. It is your responsibility. Suggestions:

  • Use the [legal] option for the component environment. This applies some formats that are specifically designed for this page:

    • Text is horizontally centered. Be sure to use manual line breaks \\ to ensure that line do not wrap or span the full textblock width.

    • The block of text appears slightly below the vertical center of the page.

    • Hyphenation is disabled. Oldstyle numerals are disabled.

    • You may use Italics and small caps. But you may not use \name or any other styling commands.

    • You may not use the block environment, because the page already is within a block.

    • Paragraphs are not indented, and there is a line gap between paragraphs.

  • Write the title, subtitle, and author.
    Format Title : Subtitle / Author.
    You may use more than one line.

  • If you use a pseudonym as author, use your real name as copyright holder.

  • You may paste the copyright symbol, or write {\copyright}. It is not enough to write (c) there.

Sample copyright page, which is very minimal:

\begin{component}[legal]
The Conspiracy Manifesto : London\\
/ Jack DeRipper\par
Copyright ©2026 Penelope Withers.\\
All Rights Reserved.\par
Published by Indescribably Bad Press,\\
Honking, New Zealand.\\
https://indescribablybadpress.com\par
ISBN 978-0-00-000000-0\par
\end{component}

Transitional Page

The copyright page is verso, and the start of your story will be recto. But you do not want these pages to face each other. Transition to the rescue, as the fifth page of front matter!

Possibilities for a transition page:

  • Epigraph. Under-used. A good epigraph can be most effective. It is a short piece of prose or poetry that directly leads to the theme of your book. Perhaps the most famous in this category is Tolkien's "One ring to rule them all..."

    Professional publishers make business arrangements so that one author can use some words from a different author. Don't do it. You are not in the business.

    Here is how to write an epigraph.

  • Dedication. Over-used. There was a time when an author would dedicate the book to some wealthy nobleman, in hopes of getting a subsidy. Obsolete, but a dedication is still used for other purposes. It is simply a short epigraph.

  • Short table of contents. Unusual, but possible. Most popular fiction has no table, unless it is a collection of short stories.

    Novelette cannot automatically create a table of contents. If you need one, you must do it yourself. Here is how.

  • Brief author's note. Unusual, but possible. Might be useful if your novel is a historical romance, and you wish to inform the reader that the story's volcanic eruption really happened in that era. Best practice is to style it like a one-page chapter.

    This is not the place to write about yourself! Any author's note must directly lead into the story.

  • Repeat of the half-title page. This is sometimes used when the author has nothing else to put there. When front matter is extended, the half-title page is reapted at as the first page of main matter.

  • If Novelette detects that \mainmatter immediately follows the [legal] page, it will insert a placeholder transition, in the form of a large design character. Not best, but it does not hurt.

Blank Page

The verso page, facing the start of your story, must be blank. Easy to do:

\blankpage

Main Matter

Front matter ends, and main matter begins, with this command: \mainmatter. Unless you used \disable{pagereset} in preamble, that resets the printable page number to arabic 1. It does not reset the internal PDF page number.

Subsequent use of \mainmatter will be ignored.
