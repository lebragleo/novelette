Overview  

Overview

The document body contains all printable content. In principle, you could write the entire story in one very large main document, containing a single, very large component. In practice, the document body is split into numerous sub-documents, each of which contains one or more small components.

Structure of Document Body

Between \begin{document} and \end{document, the document body will have this general structure:

\frontmatter
\subdoc{path/filename.tex}
\subdoc{path/filename.tex}
\mainmatter
\subdoc{path/filename.tex}
\subdoc{path/filename.tex}
\subdoc{path/filename.tex}
\subdoc{path/filename.tex}
\backmatter
\subdoc{path/filename.tex}

Front, Main, and Back Matter

The \frontmatter command must immediately follow \begin{document}. After front matter, \mainmatter must be placed before the start of your story. If there is anything after the story ends, use \backmatter.

The purpose and design of front matter, main matter, and >back matter are described on other pages.

Sub-Documents

Divide your book into numerous sub-document files. That allows you to work on small portions, and compile them individually.

Novelette does not use \include, \includeonly, or similar commands. Use sub-documents instead.

Each sub-document must contain at least one component

You may nest one sub-document within another. However, any sub-document file may only be used once, in the entire book. A nested sub-document must be between components, not in the middle of a component.

Components

All printable content must be within component environments. A sub-document must contain at least one component. You may not nest one component within another. A component may be a short as one page, such as the title page. Or, it may be as long as several chapters. The important rule is that each component begins and ends with a new page. You cannot splice the end of one component, to the beginning of another component, in the middle of a page.

You may nest a component within another

Every sub-document contains one or more components. You may also write components directly in the main document.
