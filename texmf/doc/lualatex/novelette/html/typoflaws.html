Typo Flaws

Overview

In preamble, \examine{1} activates typographic flaw detection. The default \examine{0} does not detect them.

Regardless of the setting, the examination is not performed in preview or final mode. You should finish editing before you attempt these modes.

When detection is activated, each page will be inspected by Lua after typesetting. It will find and report "typo flaws". This is time-consuming, so best practice is to wait until your writing is nearly done.

The examination is performed by Lua, using a customized version of code from the wonderful lua-typo package by Daniel Flipo.

 

What Are Typo Flaws?

TOP

Typo flaws are places where the typeset text does not look as good as it should. They are not errors; they do not prevent you from compiling in final mode. But they are areas where re‑write can improve appearance.

Typesetting flaws have nothing to do with spelling or grammar. They are places where the typeset text does not look very good, regardless of whether it makes sense.

Novelette cannot detect poor book design. That's a matter of human judgement.

 

Typo Report

TOP

As flaws are detected, a report is prepared. It has the same filename as your document (main or sub), but with typo file extension.

So, if you compile a document (main or sub) named MyWriting.tex, the typo report will be MyWriting.typo in the current directory.

Each time you compile with \examine{1}, a new report is generated. If you compile with \examine{0}, it will erase any previous report (but the typo file will still be there).

 

How to Fix Flaws

TOP

The typo examination does not change anything in your PDF. No flaws are corrected. Some flaws are more important than others; only you, the author, can decide whether to re‑write, or ignore them.

When you re-write to remove some flaws, you may introduce others. Unless you have a lot of time on your hands, do not expect to remove all flaws.

Fixing flaws is easy in English, where a native speaker has a large vocabulary of long and short words with the same meaning. The Novelette developer has done it. But fixing flaws is more difficult in languages where such things as verb declensions (Ach!), or the need for academy-approved vocabulary (Zut !) limits what you can write.

There is absolutely no way to fix flaws, other than re‑write! This is why Novelette expects that your work be original, rather than a reprint of someone else's classic. You can always re‑write your own work.

 

Automatic Flaw Prevention

TOP

During compile, Novelette attempts to avoid these typesetting flaws: one‑letter word at end of line; consecutive lines ending with hyphen. If the compile‑time code was unable to avoid those flaws, then they will be reported along with the others.

The original lua-typo code can also detect lines ending in two‑letter words. But this feature is too powerful, especially in English, where there are many two‑letter words. So it is not used.

 

List of Flaws

TOP

Some flaws are more important than others. The most important flaws, which should be corrected if possible, have uppercase names.

  • Orphan. This occurs when the first line of a paragraph appears by itself at the end of a page, with the rest of the paragraph on the following page.

    It is not very serious in popular fiction, because the reader is expected to quickly continue to the next page. The developer has seen many recent popular fiction books with orphans.

  • WIDOW. This occurs when the last line of a paragraph appears by itself at the top of a page, with the rest of the paragraph on the preceding page.

    This is very serious, even in popular fiction, because it looks bad, and is abrupt. Always re‑write to remove widows!

  • START-OF-LINE MATCH. Adjacent lines begin with the same word or the same few letters. This makes it more difficult for the reader to scan from one line to the next.

  • END-OF-LINE MATCH. Adjacent lines end with the same word or same few letters. This makes it more difficult for the reader to scan from one line to the next.

  • Adjacent lines end with hyphens. This flaw is unlikely, due to Novelette's other methods. If you see it, the paragraph was hard to typeset properly.

  • HYPHEN AT PAGE BREAK. The last word on a page is hyphenated to the next page.

  • Hyphen near end of paragraph. The next-to-last line of a paragraph is hyphenated to the last line.

  • Consecutive hyphens. Two or moare adjacent lines end with hyphen.

    This is almost a major flaw. Novelette attempts to prevent it when paragraphs are typeset. If the attempt fails, it is reported.

  • Short Last Word. Last word(s) of a paragraph are not at least 1.5x indent long.

    For best appearance, the last word(s) of a paragraph should be wider than the indent of the following paragraph.

  • SINGLE LETTER AT LINE END. Some languages (French, etc.) prohibit lines ending with a single letter. Others (English) discourage it.

    Novelette attempts to prevent this, when paragraphs are typeset. But if the attempt fails, it will be reported as a flaw.

  • Last line almost full. The last line of a paragraph almost reaches the right margin. A minor flaw.

    When the last line is noticeably shorter than the full linewidth, it gives a readers a visual cue that the next line will be a new paragraph, before they see the indent.

  • Short page. The page contains fewer then 5 lines.

    For best appearance, extended text (chapters) should not end on a page with very few lines. To fix this, you need to write more, or write less.

    Special pages, such as half-title and dedication, may trigger this flaw. But they are not really flaws; the page content is intentionally short. Ignore.

  • Excessive stretch. In order to justify the lines, too much space was added between some words.

    Fix by using more or few words, or words with more or fewer syllables.
