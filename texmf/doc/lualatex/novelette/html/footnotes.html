Novelette Documentation - Footnotes

Overview   Where Allowed   Markers/Labels   Footnote Style   Creating a Footnote   End Notes  

Overview

Novelette is intended for popular fiction, where footnotes are very rare. When used, the occasional footnote is a brief remark from the author, rather than an academic citation.

Here are some examples: (1) The story is a historical romance, with a volcanic eruption in Chapter 12. A brief footnote might simply say that there really was an eruption at that place and time. (2) The story is an “unearthed manuscript.” Occasional footnotes indicate that portions were damaged, and hard to read.

Footnotes are marked by symbols (asterisk, etc.), not numbers. If you need many footnotes, or if they must be numbered, or if they are lengthy, then Novelette is not for you.

Where Footnotes Allowed

You may not write a footnote anywhere in frontmatter or backmatter. You may not write a footnote in a component with the [single] or [legal] option. And (obviously) you may not write a footnote in page headers, or nest one footnote within another.

Otherwise (in mainmatter) you may write footnotes in display zones, in main text, and in block environments. No matter where you write them, the footnote text appears at page bottom (or the bottom of a following page).

You may not place an image within a footnote.

Markers and Labels

The marker is a raised symbol that is placed in the text. Different nations have different typographic rules, regarding how the marker is placed relative to punctuation. Novelette does not know or care; it places the marker where you write the \footnote command. Proper placement is up to you.

The label is the symbol that appears just before the footnote itself, at the bottom of the page.

Novelette does not use fake labels or markers. All of them use symbols that were designed for the purpose, in Novelette fonts.

Markers and labels are always upright, even if the nearby text is Italic. This is standard book design practice.

If you write a footnote within a display zone, it does not print a marker, and the footnote text does not have a label. This is standard book design practice. Such footnotes are meant to apply to the whole chapter, rather than to a particular sentence. For example, if Chapter 5 is named Myron and Roseanne, a footnote might say that its theme is based on Romeo and Juliet.

The order of symbols: * † ‡ § ** †† ‡‡ §§ then recycle to *.

Upon leaving a display zone and entering \maintext, the footnote count is reset (asterisk).

\footnotestyle{setting}

The text of a footnote may contain more than one paragraph. You have a choice of how to print the start of the footnote, where is label appears. This is a global setting, without local modification. If the footnote contains more than one paragraph, each additional paragraph is indented as usual.

In Preamble, you may write this command:

\footnotestyle{indent=I}

Choices for I are: near away both none (default is near).

  • near starts the first line of footnote text at the indent, with its label and space just before it (sitting within the indent).

  • away starts the first line of footnote text at the indent, but with its label at the left margin (then the space is larger).

  • both places the label at the indent, then space, then text.

  • none places the label at left margin, then space, then text. This is not quite the same as away.

Creating a Footnote: \footnote{text}

The text of a footnote may contain one or more paragraphs, separated by \par. You do not need \par at the end.

The text may contain Italics. But it may not contain small caps, or other text styles.

The text size, and line spacing, is smaller than main text. The font is specially designed for this purpose, rather than scaling the main font.

In standard LaTeX, the \footnote command allows an option. Novelette does not. If you write an option, it will be silently ignored.

^TOP

End Notes

Novelette has no provision for end notes. If your book must have them, write footnotes that say “See endnote 5” or something similar. Then design your own end notes, and place them wherever you wish. Novelette does not know, and does not care.
